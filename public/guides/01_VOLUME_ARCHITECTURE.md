# Volume Architecture

## Working identity

**Title:** *Commerce Through Digital Artifacts*  
**Subtitle:** *Eighty Structured Studies of Contemporary Indian Commerce*  
**Output type:** Edited workshop proceedings  
**Primary audience:** Undergraduate students, Commerce educators and readers interested in observable patterns in contemporary commerce

The title is deliberately descriptive. The volume is not bound by a single theory such as legitimacy. It is bound by a common method for turning public internet artifacts into small, verifiable structured datasets and bounded research notes.

## Volume-level question

> What can publicly available digital artifacts reveal about how contemporary commerce is presented, organised, transacted and experienced?

No individual note must answer that entire question. Each note contributes one small observation to it.

## Editorial shape

1. Foreword and workshop context
2. Editorial introduction
3. Common methodology and AI-use statement
4. Section I — Products, pricing and value
5. Section II — Advertising, persuasion and consumer attention
6. Section III — Consumer experience, trust and redressal
7. Section IV — Platforms, marketplaces and work
8. Section V — Entrepreneurship, MSMEs and local commerce
9. Section VI — Inclusion, accessibility and language
10. Section VII — Sustainability and responsible business
11. Section VIII — AI, data and automation in commerce
12. Editorial synthesis: what 80 small studies can and cannot establish
13. Contributor register
14. Common codebook, AI-use policy and correction policy

Each section contains ten independently authored research notes.

## Research-note contract

Each student produces:

- one 700–900 word research note;
- one research question and one principal finding;
- a dataset normally containing 12–25 public artifacts;
- one table or figure;
- one alternative explanation;
- one explicit inference boundary;
- a complete artifact register;
- a codebook and AI-work log; and
- a manuscript that conforms to the common template.

The target is a small accountable study, not an artificial imitation of a full journal article.

## What creates coherence

The volume has four binding layers:

1. **Shared terrain:** contemporary Commerce as visible through public digital artifacts.
2. **Shared method:** Claim → Corpus → Capture → Code → Check → Compose.
3. **Shared evidence contract:** artifact-level provenance, AI/human separation and reproducible coding.
4. **Shared publication form:** the same research-note structure, citation style, disclosure and editorial thresholds.

Students may use different artifact types and topic-specific variables. They may not alter the common evidence and accountability fields.

## Unit of authorship

The default unit is **one student, one note**. Pods of five are working and peer-review units, not co-authorship units. A student’s note may acknowledge pod assistance, but authorship remains individual unless two or more students made substantive intellectual contributions to the same note and the editor approves joint authorship.

Every named author must:

- approve the submitted and published version;
- accept responsibility for the accuracy of the note;
- confirm the contribution statement and AI disclosure; and
- agree to the publication route and licence selected by the institution.

## Section and room structure

| Level | Number | Responsibility |
|---|---:|---|
| Volume editor | 1 | Final scope, standards, release and corrections |
| Section editors | 8 | One section each; topic fit, cross-review and section introduction |
| Pods | 16 × 5 students | Reciprocal checking and rapid problem solving |
| Individual authors | About 80 | Evidence, dataset, analysis, writing and verification |

Section editors may be facilitators, teaching assistants or appointed students operating under facilitator supervision. Editorial authority must remain explicit.

## Topic allocation rules

1. Allocate ten topics to each section.
2. Do not allow two students to use the same research question and materially identical corpus.
3. Allow a student to refine the wording but not silently change the assigned topic family after Checkpoint 2.
4. Record topic changes in the central register with a reason.
5. Freeze the corpus rule before substantive extraction.
6. Prefer topics with stable, public organizational artifacts over personal profiles, comments or private communities.
7. If a topic fails preflight, replace it before the workshop rather than improvising during the analysis stage.

## Version and release model

| Version | Meaning | Release status |
|---|---|---|
| v0.1 | Templates and topic bank | Internal |
| v0.5 | Topic assignments and tested workflow | Internal |
| v0.9 | Same-day compiled proceedings | Private review copy |
| v1.0 | Evidence-, consent-, copyright- and style-cleared volume | Public only after editor approval |
| v1.x | Corrections that do not change the research identity | Versioned public update |
| v2.0 | Materially revised corpus, method or interpretation | New major edition |

No AI agent, student or compilation script may release a public version without the designated editor’s explicit approval.

## Minimum volume-wide outputs

- Master PDF and source package
- Eight section introductions
- Eighty accepted research notes, or a transparent explanation of omissions
- Common methodology statement
- Master artifact register
- Individual datasets and codebooks
- AI-use and contributor register
- Editorial decision log
- Licence and copyright statement
- Correction and version history

## Success test

The volume succeeds if a reader can move from any published claim to the analysis, accepted data, coding rule and source artifact—and can also see what the study does not establish.
