# Editorial Review and Acceptance Standard

## Editorial principle

The proceedings should make small claims reliably. Acceptance depends on traceability and honesty, not on obtaining a dramatic or statistically “positive” result.

An absent, mixed or contrary result is publishable when the corpus, coding and reasoning are sound.

## Decision states

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `ACCEPT_V0.9` | May enter the private same-day compilation. |
| `ACCEPT_WITH_CONDITIONS` | May enter only after listed repairs are verified. |
| `RETURN_METHOD` | Corpus, coding, validation or analysis requires repair. |
| `RETURN_MANUSCRIPT` | Evidence is usable but reporting or format requires repair. |
| `HOLD_RIGHTS_ETHICS` | Consent, privacy, copyright, conflict or legal-risk issue. |
| `WITHHOLD` | Cannot be responsibly included in the current edition. |
| `ACCEPT_V1.0` | Cleared for the approved public release. |

The editor records reasons. A student is not penalized editorially merely because a note is withheld; academic assessment and publication decisions should remain distinguishable.

## Two-pass review

### Pass A — Research integrity

Check:

- question–artifact fit;
- corpus rule and reconciliation;
- observable code definitions;
- artifact-level evidence;
- separation of agent proposals and accepted data;
- human and independent validation;
- reproducible calculations;
- correct denominator;
- claim within inference ceiling; and
- meaningful limitation.

Do not spend time polishing prose while the principal result is untraceable.

### Pass B — Publication readiness

Check:

- title, abstract and structure;
- source and artifact citations;
- quotations and image rights;
- neutral organizational descriptions;
- authorship, contribution and AI declarations;
- personal/confidential information;
- LaTeX and visual quality;
- author proof approval; and
- licence and repository metadata.

## Automatic holds

Any one of these prevents public acceptance:

- principal number cannot be reconstructed;
- artifact or quotation cannot be located;
- citation is invented, materially wrong or not opened by the author;
- included/excluded counts do not reconcile;
- codebook was materially changed after seeing results without disclosure/recode;
- agent-generated values were not checked;
- personal, confidential or restricted content is included without approval;
- unsupported causal, intent, compliance, deception or population claim;
- unresolved authorship or consent issue;
- unresolved third-party copyright/permission issue;
- missing material AI disclosure;
- fabricated, manipulated or selectively omitted evidence; or
- author refuses responsibility for the final version.

## Scored review rubric

Use scoring only after automatic holds are addressed.

| Domain | Points | Full-credit evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Question and design | 20 | Observable question, credible rival, reproducible corpus and explicit inference ceiling |
| Evidence and data | 25 | Complete provenance, locked codebook, traceable codes, reconciled counts and controlled missingness |
| AI and validation | 15 | Specific log/disclosure, separate proposal fields, 100% author checking and adequate independent check |
| Analysis and reasoning | 20 | Reproducible result, correct denominator, bounded interpretation, alternative and consequential limitation |
| Manuscript and publication | 20 | Clear structure, verified citations, readable display, declarations, rights/consent and clean proof |
| **Total** | **100** | |

Recommended public-acceptance threshold:

- total at least 75;
- at least half the available points in every domain; and
- no automatic hold.

The threshold is an editorial coordination device, not a claim of universal publication quality.

For every required change, the author records:

| Review item | Action taken | File/location | Evidence of repair | Not changed and why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | |

An author may disagree, but must answer with evidence. An AI-generated generic response is not an editorial resolution.

## Use of AI in editorial review

An editor may use an approved AI tool for mechanical checks, claim inventories or consistency scans only when confidentiality and author consent permit it. The editor must:

- disclose the tool and purpose;
- avoid uploading manuscripts to a system without an approved confidentiality basis;
- verify every actionable finding;
- prevent the tool from making final accept/reject decisions; and
- retain responsibility for the decision.

Similarity or “AI detection” scores must not be treated as proof of plagiarism or misconduct. Investigate evidence, sources and process.

## Section-level review

Each section editor checks the ten notes together for:

- duplicate or contradictory terminology;
- repeated background prose;
- consistent artifact and date descriptions;
- cross-note comparisons that are actually comparable;
- one-sided selection of only dramatic findings;
- missing notes or transparent omission statements; and
- a section introduction that synthesizes without overstating.

Section introductions must not merge unlike denominators into a false volume-wide statistic.

## Proof approval

The author receives the final typeset note and records:

```text
I approve manuscript [version/checksum] for inclusion in [volume version].
I have checked my name, affiliation, text, table/figure, citations, declarations
and artifact quotations. I consent to the approved publication and licence terms.

Name:
Date:
```

Silence, attendance or prior workbook submission is not proof approval.

## Correction policy

### Minor correction

Typographical or metadata repair that does not change evidence or interpretation. Log it and follow repository rules.

### Substantive correction

Change to data, result, quotation, method or interpretation that does not invalidate the whole note. Publish a new version and linked correction statement.

### Withdrawal or retraction

Use when evidence is fabricated, the central result is unreliable, rights/consent prohibit continued publication or another serious problem invalidates the note. The notice should:

- identify the note and version;
- state who made the decision;
- give the factual reason;
- link to the affected record where possible;
- distinguish correction from retraction; and
- avoid inflammatory language.
