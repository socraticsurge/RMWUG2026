# Manuscript and Publishing Standard

## Status of this standard

This is the house standard for the workshop proceedings. It supports responsible preparation and editorial review; it does not replace institutional policy, applicable law, publisher requirements or legal advice.

## Publication object

Each accepted contribution is a **short research note**, not a full journal article.

| Element | Standard |
|---|---|
| Main-text length | 700–900 words |
| Abstract | 60–90 words |
| Tables/figures | One principal table or figure; one small supplementary item only if essential |
| References | Normally 3–8 verified scholarly, official or authoritative sources, plus artifact citations/register |
| Dataset | Normally 12–25 artifacts |
| Citation system | Author–date through the central bibliography |
| Language | Clear professional English; translated evidence may be included with the original preserved privately |
| Release | v0.9 private review; v1.0 only after editorial authorization |

Word limits exclude references, declarations, table text and figure captions.

## Required manuscript order

### 1. Metadata block

- Study ID
- Title
- Author’s publication name
- Institutional affiliation in the approved form
- ORCID iD, optional and author-controlled
- Section/theme
- Manuscript version and date

Students should register their own ORCID only if they choose; an institution or agent must not create an ORCID record on a student’s behalf.

### 2. Title

A good title names the observable feature, artifact type and context.

**Preferred**

> Refund timelines on Indian travel-service help pages: A structured study of 18 public artifacts

**Avoid**

> The truth about refunds

Do not use accusatory, sensational or causal wording that the design cannot support.

### 3. Abstract: 60–90 words

Include, in order:

1. question;
2. corpus and collection boundary;
3. structured coding and validation method;
4. principal result with denominator; and
5. inference boundary.

Do not add citations or claims that do not appear in the note.

### 4. Introduction: 100–140 words

- Establish the Commerce problem or concept.
- Synthesize the small source conversation.
- State the research question.
- State the provisional claim or analytical expectation without pretending it was confirmed.
- Explain the bounded contribution.

### 5. Method: 150–200 words

Report:

- unit of analysis;
- artifact population and sampling rule;
- collection date/window;
- candidate, included and excluded counts;
- important code definitions;
- codebook and dataset versions;
- how the AI agent was used;
- how every retained row was checked;
- independent validation design; and
- analysis performed.

The method must allow a reader to understand how an artifact became a row and a row became a result.

### 6. Results: 180–230 words

- Reconcile corpus flow.
- Report the principal count and denominator.
- Present the table or figure.
- Describe relevant missing or unclear cases.
- Use one or two short artifact examples when they make a code understandable.
- Report observations before interpretation.

### 7. Discussion: 140–190 words

- Answer the research question at the permitted level.
- Relate the result to one or more background sources.
- Return to the plausible rival or alternative explanation.
- Distinguish evidence from interpretation.
- State what evidence should come next.

### 8. Limitations: 70–100 words

Name boundaries and their consequences. Avoid empty ritual statements such as “the sample was small” without explaining what that changes.

### 9. Declarations

Every note includes:

- AI-use disclosure;
- data-availability statement;
- conflict-of-interest statement;
- funding/support statement;
- contributor statement; and
- author accountability declaration.

## Reporting numerical results

- Report counts with percentages: `7 of 18 artifacts (39%)`.
- Normally use whole percentages for samples below 100.
- State the denominator whenever it can change.
- Do not exclude `UNCLEAR` cases silently.
- Label a statistic as mean, median, range or count.
- Do not use inferential significance language merely because software supplied a p-value.
- Explain derived variables or provide a reproducible calculation.
- Do not imply that a non-probability artifact sample represents all organizations or consumers.

## Tables and figures

Every item must:

- be cited in the text;
- answer a specific question;
- identify the sample or denominator;
- use the locked dataset;
- show missing/unclear cases when material;
- have a self-contained caption;
- avoid decorative 3D effects and false precision; and
- remain readable in grayscale and at the final page width.

Use a table when exact values matter. Use a simple bar chart or dot plot when a comparison is easier to see visually. Pie charts, word clouds and AI-generated decorative images are not accepted as evidence displays.

## Quotations and artifact evidence

- Quote exactly and preserve spelling unless a correction is shown transparently.
- Identify the artifact through its register ID and citation.
- Use the minimum language needed to support the code.
- Ordinarily publish no more than 25 consecutive words from one artifact without documented permission or another editor-approved basis.
- Do not publish full screenshots by default.
- A screenshot or copyrighted image requires a recorded licence, permission or editorially confirmed reuse basis.
- Never assume that public availability means unrestricted republication.

## Organization names and criticism

Organizations may be named when the artifact is public and naming is methodologically necessary. Authors must:

- describe the artifact and code, not attack an organization;
- avoid attributing motive or dishonesty;
- distinguish “not found under this protocol” from “does not exist”;
- give dates because pages change;
- avoid legal-compliance conclusions unless the editor has approved the design and review; and
- use neutral correction language if an artifact was misread.

## Sources and references

- Use verified author–date citations managed through the central bibliography.
- Prefer DOI links for scholarly sources and official canonical URLs for standards or policies.
- Cite the source that carries the claim, not a search result or AI summary.
- Verify author/organization, title, year, publisher/venue, DOI/URL and access date where appropriate.
- Do not cite an AI tool as the substantive authority for a factual or scholarly claim.
- Artifacts used as data belong in the artifact register; cite individual artifacts in text only when quoted or discussed specifically.
- Use one canonical bibliography key per source across the volume.

## Authorship and contribution

Authorship conveys both credit and accountability. Every named author must:

1. make a substantive intellectual contribution;
2. participate in drafting or critical revision;
3. approve the final version; and
4. accept accountability for the work.

The volume records contributor roles using the CRediT vocabulary where applicable. CRediT describes contributions; it does not itself determine who qualifies as an author.

AI tools cannot be authors because they cannot approve the work or accept responsibility.

## AI-use disclosure

State:

- tool and model/version as displayed;
- dates or period of material use;
- tasks performed;
- whether AI proposed codes, calculations, prose or figures;
- human validation performed;
- material error/correction, where relevant; and
- human responsibility for the retained work.

If AI was not materially used, state that. Do not disguise AI-generated analysis as manual work.

## Conflicts, support and acknowledgements

Disclose relevant financial, employment, personal or organizational relationships that a reader could reasonably think affected selection or interpretation. Record material institutional, teacher, peer or technical support.

Obtain permission before naming someone in an acknowledgement when the wording could imply endorsement.

## Privacy and research ethics

- Use public organizational artifacts by default.
- Exclude unnecessary names, handles, contact details and personal data.
- Do not reproduce personal reviews or social comments unless the editor has approved the method and safeguards.
- Do not upload confidential or restricted content to an external AI tool.
- Do not infer protected identities from names or images.
- Institutional and applicable Indian data-protection rules take precedence.

## Licence and rights

The institution/editor must choose the volume licence after confirming:

- student publication consent;
- who owns or is authorized to license the manuscript and compilation;
- whether third-party text, data and images can be redistributed;
- whether commercial reuse should be permitted; and
- whether the dataset has a separate licence.

Do not automatically apply a Creative Commons licence. A CC licence grants advance permissions and cannot be used to license third-party material the authors do not control. Clearly mark excluded third-party material.

## Publication package

The public v1.0 package should normally contain:

- compiled PDF;
- citation metadata;
- table of contents and contributor register;
- common method and AI policy;
- public artifact register;
- approved coded datasets and codebooks;
- correction/version history; and
- licence and third-party-material statement.

Private page captures, restricted records and unnecessary personal data do not belong in the public package.

## Proof and release

Before public release, each author receives the typeset note and confirms:

- name and affiliation;
- title and text;
- table/figure values;
- citations;
- AI and contributor statements;
- any organization names and excerpts; and
- publication consent.

Silence is not proof approval. The editor records affirmative approval or withholds the note.

## Corrections and versioning

- Minor metadata repairs may be logged without changing the research files where the repository permits it.
- File changes require a new version.
- Corrections must identify the affected note, original statement, corrected statement, reason, date and responsible editor.
- Material evidence or analysis failures may require withdrawal from the current edition or a clearly linked retraction notice.
- Notices must be factual and non-inflammatory.
- Cite a specific version when reproducibility depends on exact files.

## Automatic publication holds

A note cannot enter v1.0 while any of these remain:

- missing author approval or consent;
- untraceable principal result;
- invented or unverified citation;
- unresolved corpus/count discrepancy;
- AI-proposed values presented as human-verified when they were not;
- confidential or prohibited data;
- unsupported accusation, causal claim, intent claim or legal conclusion;
- unresolved copyright/permission problem;
- missing AI disclosure; or
- unresolved critical editorial query.
