# Topic Bank: 80 Artifact-Based Commerce Studies

## How to use this bank

Each row is a bounded research opportunity, not a predetermined conclusion. Students should convert the question into a provisional claim and plausible rival, then follow the common method.

Default design unless a topic card is adapted:

- **Unit:** one organization × one eligible public artifact;
- **Sample:** 12–25 artifacts, normally 15–20;
- **Collection:** a declared period during the workshop;
- **Evidence:** public organizational pages or other editor-approved public artifacts;
- **Analysis:** descriptive counts, percentages, comparisons or simple associations;
- **Ceiling:** no causal, population-wide, legal-compliance or intent claim.

The facilitator must preflight the final topics. Availability on the open web can change.

## Section I — Products, pricing and value

| ID | Research question | Eligible artifacts and sampling idea | Suggested coded fields | Important boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PV01 | How are reference prices and discounts framed on online fashion product pages? | Select one product category; systematically sample product pages from 4–6 declared retailers. | Listed price, current price, computed discount, displayed discount, framing words, expiry cue. | A displayed discount does not establish the product’s historical selling price or consumer saving. |
| PV02 | Do price endings differ across product categories in online grocery listings? | Sample a fixed number of visible listings from 3–5 grocery categories on one declared date. | Price, last digit, category, pack size, promotion flag, retailer. | Do not infer pricing strategy or consumer response from endings alone. |
| PV03 | How clearly do published delivery-fee pages explain the final charge? | Compare public delivery, fee or help pages across food, grocery or retail services. | Base fee stated, variable fee, minimum order, surge/weather caveat, location dependency, example. | Policy descriptions may not match every personalized checkout. |
| PV04 | How do subscription services visually and verbally differentiate plan tiers? | Use public pricing pages from consumer or small-business subscription services. | Number of tiers, default/emphasized tier, monthly/annual framing, feature count, savings claim, cancellation link. | Visual emphasis is observable; its effect on purchase is not. |
| PV05 | How visible are free-trial conditions and post-trial prices? | Sample public offer or pricing pages that advertise a trial. | Trial length, payment method required, renewal price, cancellation timing, reminder statement, disclosure location. | Do not label a design deceptive without a predeclared legal or behavioural standard. |
| PV06 | How comparable are pack sizes and unit values in marketplace listings? | Choose one packaged-goods category and sample comparable listings. | Pack count, net quantity, displayed unit price, computable unit price, bundle, discount. | Compare only genuinely comparable units; do not treat missing unit price as evidence of intent. |
| PV07 | How are EMI or pay-later options framed on high-value product pages? | Sample electronics, appliances or travel booking pages with visible financing information. | Monthly amount, total cost, interest/fee statement, tenure, eligibility caveat, lender named. | A product page is not the complete regulated credit agreement. |
| PV08 | When does a bundle offer provide a verifiable monetary advantage? | Sample declared bundle or combo pages and their separately priced components. | Bundle price, component total, computed saving, claimed saving, unavailable component, non-price benefit. | Missing or changing component prices can make the comparison indeterminate. |
| PV09 | What kinds of value are promised in public product-warranty descriptions? | Select one durable-goods category and compare manufacturer warranty pages. | Duration, parts/labour, exclusions, registration, remedy, geographic limit, extended option. | Warranty language describes a promise; it does not measure claim fulfilment. |
| PV10 | How do premium consumer products justify higher prices? | Sample premium and standard variants within one category or comparable brand pages. | Ingredient/material claim, craft/origin, performance, scarcity, certification, quantified evidence, price ratio. | Do not conclude that the premium is justified to consumers; report the public justification offered. |

## Section II — Advertising, persuasion and consumer attention

## Topic preflight test

Before assignment, the facilitator must answer **yes** to all of the following:

- [ ] At least 15 eligible artifacts appear likely to be publicly accessible.
- [ ] The artifact type can be defined without relying on private accounts or personal data.
- [ ] The proposed variables are observable rather than speculative.
- [ ] A meaningful “not present” or `UNCLEAR` value is possible.
- [ ] The likely analysis fits descriptive or simple comparative methods.
- [ ] The topic avoids medical, legal or financial advice.
- [ ] The wording does not presume wrongdoing, deception, discrimination or intent.
- [ ] The student can complete collection, checking and analysis within the workshop.
- [ ] The topic does not duplicate another student’s question and corpus.
- [ ] The section editor has recorded the final wording and topic owner.

## Adaptation rule

A student may narrow an industry, geography, product category or artifact type. Any change must retain the assigned section, remain answerable through public artifacts and be approved before the corpus is locked. The student may not change the question after seeing an inconvenient result merely to obtain a stronger story.
